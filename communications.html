Section 7 · roughly 9 to 13 marks

Communications

Often the heaviest core section, and the one that leans most on probability and Fourier transforms. Learn each modulation scheme as a picture in time and a picture in frequency, then the formulas are easy to rebuild.

Standard references: Haykin, Communication Systems; Lathi and Ding, Modern Digital and Analog Communication Systems; Proakis and Salehi, Communication Systems Engineering.

Random processes

Syllabus: autocorrelation and power spectral density, properties of white noise, filtering of random signals through LTI systems.

Notes

  • A random process \(X(t)\) is a family of waveforms, one per outcome. Mean \(\mu_X(t)=E[X(t)]\). Autocorrelation \(R_X(t_1,t_2)=E[X(t_1)X(t_2)]\).
  • Wide-sense stationary (WSS): the mean is constant and the autocorrelation depends only on the lag \(\tau=t_1-t_2\). Ergodic: time averages over one waveform equal the ensemble averages.
  • Properties of \(R_X(\tau)\) for a real WSS process: \(R_X(0)=E[X^2]\) is the total average power. It is even, \(R_X(-\tau)=R_X(\tau)\). Its maximum is at zero, \(|R_X(\tau)|\le R_X(0)\). If there is no periodic part, \(R_X(\infty)=\mu_X^2\), so the variance is \(R_X(0)-R_X(\infty)\).
  • Wiener–Khinchin theorem: the power spectral density is the Fourier transform of the autocorrelation, \(S_X(f)=\mathcal F\{R_X(\tau)\}\). It is real, even and non-negative, and \(\displaystyle\int S_X(f)\,df=R_X(0)\).
  • White noise: \(S(f)=\dfrac{N_0}{2}\) at all frequencies (two-sided), so \(R(\tau)=\dfrac{N_0}{2}\delta(\tau)\). Any two different instants are uncorrelated. Its total power is infinite, so it is an idealisation that becomes physical only after filtering.
  • Through an LTI system \(H(f)\): \(\mu_Y=\mu_XH(0)\) and \[S_Y(f)=|H(f)|^2\,S_X(f)\]
  • White noise through an ideal low-pass filter of bandwidth \(B\): output power \(N_0B\), and \(R_Y(\tau)=N_0B\,\operatorname{sinc}(2B\tau)\). Samples taken \(\dfrac{1}{2B}\) apart are uncorrelated.
  • White noise through an RC low-pass filter: output power \(\dfrac{N_0}{4RC}\). The noise-equivalent bandwidth is \(\dfrac{1}{4RC}=\dfrac\pi2f_{3dB}\).
  • A Gaussian process stays Gaussian through any linear system. For jointly Gaussian variables, uncorrelated implies independent.
  • Narrow-band noise: \(n(t)=n_I(t)\cos\omega_ct-n_Q(t)\sin\omega_ct\). \(n_I\) and \(n_Q\) each have the same power as \(n\). The envelope is Rayleigh distributed and the phase is uniform. With a sinusoid added the envelope is Rician.
  • Thermal noise of a resistor: available power \(kTB\), open-circuit mean-square voltage \(4kTRB\).

Seen in real life

The hiss of an FM radio tuned between stations is thermal noise from the receiver’s front end, shaped by its filters. Averaging \(N\) readings of a noisy sensor reduces the noise by \(\sqrt N\) because averaging is a low-pass filter, and it only works when successive readings are uncorrelated, which is the property the autocorrelation plot shows.

Common traps

  • \(N_0/2\) is the two-sided density. The power in a bandwidth \(B\) at baseband is \(\dfrac{N_0}{2}\times2B=N_0B\).
  • \(R_X(0)\) is total power, including the DC part \(\mu_X^2\). Variance is what remains after subtracting it.
  • When the density is given against \(\omega\), integrate with \(\dfrac{d\omega}{2\pi}\).
  • Uncorrelated implies independent only for Gaussian variables.

Practice

White noise with \(N_0/2=10^{-6}\text{ W/Hz}\) passes through an ideal low-pass filter of bandwidth 5 kHz. Find the output noise power.

\(P=N_0B=2\times10^{-6}\times5000=10\text{ mW}\).

A WSS process has \(R_X(\tau)=16+9e^{-2|\tau|}\). Find its mean, total power and variance.

\(R_X(\infty)=16=\mu_X^2\), so the mean is \(\pm4\). Total power \(R_X(0)=25\). Variance \(=25-16=9\).

Analog communications

Syllabus: amplitude modulation and demodulation, angle modulation and demodulation, spectra of AM and FM, superheterodyne receivers.

Notes

  • AM (DSB with carrier): \(s(t)=A_c[1+\mu\,m(t)]\cos\omega_ct\) with \(|m(t)|\le1\). From the envelope: \(\mu=\dfrac{A_{max}-A_{min}}{A_{max}+A_{min}}\).
  • For a single tone: a carrier of amplitude \(A_c\) and two sidebands of amplitude \(\dfrac{\mu A_c}{2}\) at \(f_c\pm f_m\). Bandwidth \(2f_m\). \[P_t=P_c\left(1+\frac{\mu^2}{2}\right),\qquad\eta=\frac{\mu^2}{2+\mu^2}\le33.3\,\%\]
  • Several tones: \(\mu_t=\sqrt{\mu_1^2+\mu_2^2+\dots}\).
  • DSB-SC: \(m(t)\cos\omega_ct\). Bandwidth \(2f_m\), no carrier power, needs coherent detection. A phase error \(\phi\) in the local carrier scales the output by \(\cos\phi\). SSB: one sideband only, bandwidth \(f_m\). VSB: one sideband plus a vestige of the other, used for television.
  • Envelope detector (needs \(\mu\le1\)): choose \(\dfrac{1}{f_c}\ll RC\ll\dfrac{1}{f_m}\). To follow the envelope without diagonal clipping: \(RC\le\dfrac{\sqrt{1-\mu^2}}{2\pi f_m\mu}\).
  • Angle modulation: \(s(t)=A_c\cos[\omega_ct+\phi(t)]\). PM: \(\phi=k_p\,m(t)\). FM: instantaneous frequency \(f_i=f_c+k_f\,m(t)\), so \(\phi=2\pi k_f\displaystyle\int m\,dt\).
  • Tone-modulated FM: deviation \(\Delta f=k_fA_m\), modulation index \(\beta=\dfrac{\Delta f}{f_m}\), and \[s(t)=A_c\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}J_n(\beta)\cos[2\pi(f_c+nf_m)t]\] Total power is \(A_c^2/2\) for every \(\beta\), since \(\sum J_n^2(\beta)=1\).
  • Carson’s rule: \(B\approx2(\Delta f+f_m)=2(\beta+1)f_m\). Narrow-band FM (\(\beta\ll1\)) has bandwidth \(2f_m\).
  • A frequency multiplier by \(n\) multiplies \(f_c\), \(\Delta f\) and \(\beta\) by \(n\). A mixer shifts \(f_c\) only.
  • FM demodulation: frequency discriminator (differentiator followed by envelope detector) or phase-locked loop. Pre-emphasis at the transmitter and de-emphasis at the receiver reduce high-frequency noise.
  • Output SNR relative to baseband (figure of merit): DSB-SC and SSB: 1. AM with envelope detection, single tone: \(\dfrac{\mu^2}{2+\mu^2}\). FM, single tone: \(\dfrac32\beta^2\), above threshold.
  • Superheterodyne receiver: RF amplifier, mixer with local oscillator, IF amplifier, detector. \(f_{LO}=f_s+f_{IF}\) (oscillator above the signal). Image frequency \(=f_s+2f_{IF}\), rejected by the RF stage. The fixed IF stage gives the selectivity. Typical IF: 455 kHz for AM, 10.7 MHz for FM.

Seen in real life

A car radio is a superheterodyne receiver: turning the dial changes only the local oscillator, and one fixed filter does all the station selection. FM sounds cleaner than AM because most noise changes a signal’s amplitude, which an FM receiver ignores. Broadcast FM spends about 200 kHz per station to buy that quality, twenty times what AM uses.

Common traps

  • AM power uses \(\mu^2/2\). The sideband amplitude uses \(\mu/2\). Do not swap them.
  • With the local oscillator above the signal, the image is above the signal by \(2f_{IF}\). If the question puts the oscillator below, the image is below.
  • FM bandwidth depends on both \(\Delta f\) and \(f_m\). Doubling the message amplitude doubles \(\Delta f\), not the bandwidth.
  • In PM the deviation is proportional to \(f_m\). In FM it is not.

Practice

An AM transmitter has a carrier power of 1 kW and \(\mu=0.6\). Find the total power, the power in each sideband and the efficiency.

\(P_t=1000(1+0.18)=1180\text{ W}\). Each sideband carries 90 W. \(\eta=\dfrac{0.36}{2.36}=15.3\,\%\).

An FM signal has \(\Delta f=75\text{ kHz}\) and \(f_m=15\text{ kHz}\). Find \(\beta\) and the Carson bandwidth. What do they become after a frequency tripler?

\(\beta=5\). \(B=2(75+15)=180\text{ kHz}\).

After tripling: \(\Delta f=225\text{ kHz}\), \(\beta=15\), \(B=2(225+15)=480\text{ kHz}\). The message frequency is unchanged.

A superheterodyne AM receiver with a 455 kHz IF is tuned to 1000 kHz, oscillator above the signal. Find the oscillator and image frequencies.

\(f_{LO}=1455\text{ kHz}\). Image \(=1000+910=1910\text{ kHz}\).

Information theory

Syllabus: entropy, source coding, mutual information and channel capacity theorem.

Notes

  • Information in an outcome of probability \(p\): \(I=\log_2\dfrac1p\) bits. Rare events carry more information.
  • Entropy is the average information per symbol: \[H(X)=-\sum_ip_i\log_2p_i\ \text{bits/symbol}\] \(0\le H\le\log_2M\) for \(M\) symbols, with the maximum when all are equally likely.
  • Source coding theorem: the average codeword length \(L\) of any uniquely decodable code satisfies \(L\ge H\). Efficiency \(=H/L\). A Huffman code is the optimal prefix code and achieves \(H\le L\lt H+1\).
  • Kraft inequality: a prefix code with lengths \(l_i\) exists only if \(\sum2^{-l_i}\le1\).
  • Joint and conditional entropy: \(H(X,Y)=H(X)+H(Y|X)\). Mutual information: \[I(X;Y)=H(X)-H(X|Y)=H(Y)-H(Y|X)=H(X)+H(Y)-H(X,Y)\ge0\]
  • Channel capacity is the largest mutual information over all input distributions. Binary symmetric channel with crossover probability \(p\): \(C=1-H(p)\). Binary erasure channel with erasure probability \(\alpha\): \(C=1-\alpha\).
  • Shannon–Hartley theorem for a band-limited Gaussian channel: \[C=B\log_2\left(1+\frac SN\right)\ \text{bits/s},\qquad N=N_0B\]
  • As \(B\to\infty\): \(C\to1.44\,\dfrac{S}{N_0}\). Error-free communication is impossible below \(\dfrac{E_b}{N_0}=\ln2\), which is −1.59 dB.
  • Channel coding theorem: if the information rate \(R=rH\) (symbol rate times entropy) is below \(C\), codes exist that make the error probability as small as desired. Above \(C\), none do.

Seen in real life

ZIP files, PNG images and MP3 audio all end with an entropy coder that gives short codes to common patterns. Morse code did the same by hand in the 1830s: “E”, the most frequent letter in English, is a single dot. Wi-Fi and 4G/5G constantly measure the signal-to-noise ratio and switch modulation and coding to stay just under the Shannon capacity of the moment.

Common traps

  • The capacity formula needs \(S/N\) as a ratio. Convert from dB first.
  • Use \(\log_2\). If your calculator has only \(\log_{10}\): \(\log_2x=3.322\log_{10}x\).
  • A Huffman code is not unique, but its average length is.
  • Doubling the bandwidth also doubles the noise power, so capacity less than doubles for a fixed signal power.

Practice

A source emits four symbols with probabilities \(\tfrac12,\tfrac14,\tfrac18,\tfrac18\). Find the entropy and the average length of a Huffman code.

\(H=\tfrac12(1)+\tfrac14(2)+\tfrac18(3)+\tfrac18(3)=1.75\text{ bits/symbol}\).

Huffman lengths are 1, 2, 3, 3, giving \(L=1.75\). Efficiency is 100 % because every probability is a power of \(\tfrac12\).

A channel has a bandwidth of 4 kHz and a signal-to-noise ratio of 1023. Find its capacity.

\(C=4000\log_2(1024)=4000\times10=40\text{ kbit/s}\).

Digital communications

Syllabus: PCM, DPCM, digital modulation schemes (ASK, PSK, FSK, QAM), bandwidth, inter-symbol interference, MAP, ML detection, matched filter receiver, SNR and BER.

Notes

  • PCM: sample at \(f_s\ge2W\), quantise to \(L=2^n\) levels, encode \(n\) bits per sample. Bit rate \(R_b=nf_s\). Minimum transmission bandwidth \(\dfrac{R_b}{2}\).
  • Uniform quantiser with step \(\Delta\): noise power \(\dfrac{\Delta^2}{12}\). Full-scale sine: \(\text{SQNR}=6.02n+1.76\text{ dB}\). Each extra bit adds 6 dB. Companding (µ-law, A-law) keeps the ratio roughly constant for quiet and loud speech.
  • DPCM quantises the difference between a sample and its prediction, which has a smaller range, so fewer bits are needed. Delta modulation is 1-bit DPCM: slope overload occurs unless \(\Delta\,f_s\ge\max\left|\dfrac{dm}{dt}\right|\), which for a tone is \(2\pi f_mA_m\). Too large a step causes granular noise instead.
  • Inter-symbol interference is avoided when the overall pulse is zero at every other sampling instant (Nyquist criterion). Minimum baseband bandwidth \(\dfrac{R_b}{2}\) with a sinc pulse. A raised-cosine pulse with roll-off \(\alpha\) needs \(\dfrac{R_b}{2}(1+\alpha)\) at baseband and twice that after modulation.
  • Matched filter: \(h(t)=s(T-t)\). It maximises the SNR at the sampling instant, and the maximum is \(\dfrac{2E}{N_0}\), which depends only on the pulse energy, not on its shape. A correlator gives the same result.
  • MAP detection picks the symbol with the largest posterior probability \(P(s_i|r)\) and minimises the error probability. ML detection picks the largest likelihood \(p(r|s_i)\). They coincide when the symbols are equally likely. In Gaussian noise, ML means choosing the nearest constellation point.
  • Two equally likely signals a distance \(d\) apart in Gaussian noise: \(P_e=Q\!\left(\dfrac{d}{\sqrt{2N_0}}\right)\), where \(Q(x)=\tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(x/\sqrt2)\).

  • \(E_s=E_b\log_2M\). With Gray coding, bit error rate ≈ symbol error rate ÷ \(\log_2M\).
  • \(M\)-ary PSK and QAM save bandwidth and cost power. \(M\)-ary orthogonal FSK saves power and costs bandwidth.

Seen in real life

A Wi-Fi link close to the router uses 256-QAM or 1024-QAM, packing 8 or 10 bits into each symbol. Walk away and the signal-to-noise ratio falls, the dots of the constellation smear into each other, and the link steps down to 64-QAM, 16-QAM, QPSK and finally BPSK. Landline telephony digitises speech as 8000 samples per second × 8 bits, which is the 64 kbit/s channel that all later telecom rates were built from.

Common traps

  • Check whether the question gives energy per bit or per symbol, and whether noise density is \(N_0\) or \(N_0/2\).
  • QPSK has the same bit error rate as BPSK, but about twice the symbol error rate.
  • Raised-cosine bandwidth at baseband is \(\dfrac{R_s}{2}(1+\alpha)\). For a modulated (passband) signal it is \(R_s(1+\alpha)\). For \(M\)-ary signalling use the symbol rate \(R_s=R_b/\log_2M\).
  • The matched-filter output SNR does not depend on the pulse shape, only on its energy.

Practice

Speech band-limited to 3.4 kHz is sampled at 8 kHz and quantised to 256 levels. Find the bit rate, the minimum transmission bandwidth and the SQNR for a full-scale sine.

\(n=8\). \(R_b=64\text{ kbit/s}\). Minimum bandwidth \(=32\text{ kHz}\). SQNR \(=6.02\times8+1.76=49.9\text{ dB}\).

Binary data at 1 Mbit/s uses raised-cosine pulses with \(\alpha=0.5\). Find the bandwidth at baseband and for BPSK.

Baseband: \(\dfrac{1\text{ MHz}}{2}(1.5)=750\text{ kHz}\). BPSK: \(1.5\text{ MHz}\).

Error correction

Syllabus: fundamentals of error correction, Hamming codes, CRC.

Notes

  • An \((n,k)\) block code maps \(k\) message bits to \(n\) code bits. Rate \(=k/n\). The Hamming distance between two words is the number of positions in which they differ.
  • With minimum distance \(d_{min}\): detects up to \(d_{min}-1\) errors, corrects up to \(t=\left\lfloor\dfrac{d_{min}-1}{2}\right\rfloor\) errors. For a linear code, \(d_{min}\) equals the smallest weight of any non-zero codeword.
  • Hamming bound: to correct \(t\) errors, \(2^{n-k}\ge\sum_{i=0}^{t}\binom ni\).
  • Hamming codes: \(n=2^m-1\), \(k=n-m\), \(d_{min}=3\). Examples: (7,4), (15,11), (31,26). They correct any single error and meet the Hamming bound with equality (perfect codes). Adding one overall parity bit gives \(d_{min}=4\): single-error correction with double-error detection.
  • Systematic form: generator \(G=[\,I_k\;|\;P\,]\), parity-check \(H=[\,P^T\;|\;I_{n-k}\,]\), and \(GH^T=0\). Codeword \(c=mG\).
  • Syndrome: \(s=rH^T=eH^T\). It depends only on the error pattern. Zero syndrome: accept. For a single error, the syndrome equals the column of \(H\) at the error position.
  • A single parity bit detects any odd number of errors. A repetition code \((n,1)\) has \(d_{min}=n\).
  • CRC with generator polynomial \(g(x)\) of degree \(r\): append \(r\) zeros to the message, divide by \(g(x)\) using modulo-2 arithmetic, and transmit the message followed by the \(r\)-bit remainder. The receiver divides the whole frame by \(g(x)\). A non-zero remainder means an error.
  • A CRC detects every burst error of length \(r\) or less, every single-bit error (if \(g\) has at least two terms), and every odd number of errors if \((x+1)\) divides \(g(x)\).

Seen in real life

Server memory (ECC RAM) stores every word with a Hamming-type code so that a bit flipped by a cosmic ray is corrected silently. Every Ethernet frame, ZIP file and CAN message carries a CRC, and a frame that fails the check is discarded and sent again. A QR code still scans with a corner torn off because a stronger relative of these codes (Reed–Solomon) rebuilds the missing part.

Common traps

  • Detecting \(d_{min}-1\) errors and correcting \(t\) errors are alternatives. A code used for correction detects fewer.
  • Modulo-2 arithmetic has no carries or borrows. Addition and subtraction are both XOR.
  • Parity bits of the classic Hamming layout sit at positions 1, 2, 4, 8, counted from 1.
  • A CRC detects errors. It does not correct them.

Practice

A block code has \(d_{min}=5\). How many errors can it detect? How many can it correct?

Detect up to 4. Correct up to \(\lfloor4/2\rfloor=2\).

The message 1001 is protected by a CRC with \(g(x)=x^3+x+1\) (1011). Find the transmitted frame.

Append three zeros: 1001000. Divide by 1011 with XOR:

\(1001\oplus1011=0010\), leaving 0010000. The next leading 1 aligns 1011 under 1000: \(1000\oplus1011=0011\), leaving 0000110. The remainder is 110.

Transmitted frame: 1001110. Check: dividing 1001110 by 1011 leaves 000.
